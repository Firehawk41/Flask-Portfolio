About Me

Jamie Thomson

When I started university, I seriously considered studying software engineering. I ended up studying Physics and working in labs, but spent the next 15 years learning how to write code myself.

Throughout my career as a chemist, I've always looked for ways to improve my workflow with different code. In my current position, we used state-of-the-art equipment and techniques but relied on Excel workbooks and lots of copy/paste to move and analyze data. I combed Stack Overflow and started making VBA macros for myself to automate tedious tasks and pretty soon they were officially integrated into our lab workflow. The first big paradigm shift was signing up for Codecademy and starting to learn the strengths of different languages and what their capabilities were. Learning the theory behind code, such as single responsibility principle really expanded my thinking on what was possible for me to create. It allowed me to write programs rather than just macros.

Paired with the structured learning of Codecademy was the ability to fuel my curiosity that AI provides. I started using ChatGPT pretty early on and I was able to learn how to design code with architecture. That's when I started actually engineering solutions rather than just making code that worked. I've recently moved from ChatGPT to Claude and have used it to complete a refactor of a VBA invoice program and a port to Python (more information here). Along the way, I've learned that the old adage is still true: "rubbish in, rubbish out". Prompt coding and prompt design is a skill.

I'm looking for a software engineering role to further my passion for designing and building new software. In coding, I've always worked by myself because I'm the only programmer on the team, but I'd love an opportunity to learn from a team of experienced developers. I work closely with my lab team and know that "iron sharpens iron". Long term I'm drawn to building custom tools for technical domains — the kind of software that replaces a Word invoice template and a Save-As habit.

My lab experience has added to my skillset: I'm able to break complex problems down into small, repeatable steps, build in controls and document the process so the results are reproducible. I took the long way around to software engineering, but I'm a better engineer for the additional skills it brought.
